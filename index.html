Building Dashboards with AI
Start here — your guide to AI-powered data storytelling
A new way to work with data

Build dashboards that tell stories — with AI as your co-pilot

You don't need to be a developer or a data scientist. You need a clear question, a dataset, and the ability to describe what you want in plain English. This guide shows you the full journey — from blank page to polished, interactive dashboard.

Why AI changes everything for dashboards

Building a dashboard used to require a developer, a BI tool license, and weeks of back-and-forth. AI collapses that into a conversation. Here's what's actually different now:

⚡
Speed: hours, not weeks
A prototype that would take a developer two days now takes two hours of prompting. You can validate ideas before committing resources.
🗣️
Plain English is enough
You don't write code — you describe what you want. "Add a donut chart showing revenue by region, clicking it should filter the table" is a complete instruction.
🔄
Iteration is free
Change your mind about a chart type? Swap dark mode in? Add a new filter? Each is a single sentence. There's no cost to exploring.
📖
You stay in control of the story
AI handles the code. You own the narrative — what to highlight, what anomalies to call out, what decision your dashboard is meant to support.

The journey — what it actually looks like

Click each step to open it. Work through them in order — each one gives you a reflection prompt to make the next step sharper.

Click a step to begin 0 of 6
💬
Step 1
Define your story before touching data
▼
Start with a blank document, not a spreadsheet. Write down: who reads this dashboard, what decision it supports, and the single most important thing you want them to walk away knowing. This becomes the brief you give the AI — and a clear brief produces dramatically better output than vague instructions.
✦ Reflection prompt
"Who is the primary reader of my dashboard — and what single decision do I want to make easier for them? Write it in one sentence."
A one-sentence brief = a better first prompt to the AI
📂
Step 2
Describe your data — or ask for synthetic data first
▼
Paste a sample of your data into Claude — even just 5–10 rows of a CSV — and describe what each column means. No data yet? Ask for synthetic data that matches your schema. Prototyping with fake data is faster and lets you validate the design before the real data is ready. Don't wait for perfect data to start.
✦ Reflection prompt
"What are my column names and what does each one actually measure? Are there any columns I'm tracking that don't directly support the decision I defined in Step 1?"
Irrelevant columns = chart clutter — cut them now
🏗️
Step 3
Build the first version fast — expect it to be ugly
▼
Ask for the full layout in one prompt: header, KPI cards, one chart, one table. Don't try to get it perfect — get it working. You'll see things you didn't know you wanted once it's in front of you. The first version is a conversation starter, not a deliverable. Request KPI row → primary chart → data table, in that order. Don't add filters or interactivity until the core is solid.
✦ Reflection prompt
"Looking at my first version: what is the most prominent thing on the page? Is that the most important metric — or did the AI just make it big by default?"
If the biggest thing isn't the most important thing, fix it first
🔁
Step 4
Iterate one feature at a time
▼
This is where most people go wrong: they ask for ten things at once and get a tangled mess. Instead, add one feature per prompt. "Now add a region filter that updates all charts." Then: "Now make the table sortable." Each change is a sentence. Say "confirm before continuing" to stay in control. If you find yourself writing a numbered list of 6 things to add, split it into 6 separate messages.
✦ Reflection prompt
"What's the one interaction that would save my audience the most time — a filter, a search, a sort, a date range? Add that one thing next, nothing else."
One feature at a time prevents compounding errors
✨
Step 5
Polish the story, not just the visuals
▼
Once the mechanics work, go back to your brief. Are the right things prominent? Add annotations to your anomalies. Change chart titles from descriptive ("Revenue by Quarter") to declarative ("Q3 revenue fell 18% — supply chain delays"). A dashboard without context is just a grid of numbers. Ask the AI: "suggest 3 things that would make this story clearer to a non-technical reader" — you'll get ideas you hadn't considered.
✦ Reflection prompt
"If a busy executive had 30 seconds with my dashboard, what would they see and conclude? Is that the right conclusion — or am I accidentally hiding the most important insight?"
The 30-second test reveals what you've buried
🚀
Step 6
Ship it — then listen
▼
Share the dashboard with your audience. The questions they ask — "can you break this down by X?", "what happened in March?" — are your next iteration list. Save every prompt you used to build this. When you need to rebuild or hand off, you have the recipe. With AI, adding a new filter based on feedback takes minutes, not a Jira ticket.
✦ Reflection prompt
"What's the first question my audience will ask that I can't currently answer from this dashboard? Write it down — that's the first item in your next iteration."
Feedback is free product discovery — collect it actively

Mindset shifts that make you better at this

Working with AI is a skill. These are the shifts that separate people who get great results from those who get mediocre ones.

🎯
Be specific about outcomes, not steps
"Make it look better" gets you nothing. "Make the KPI cards larger and move the chart title to the left" gets you exactly what you want. Describe the outcome you can see, not the process to get there.
🧪
Treat every output as a draft
The AI's first attempt is a starting point, not a final answer. Read it critically. What's missing? What's wrong? What surprised you? Your reaction to the draft is the next prompt.
📐
Constraints produce creativity
Tell the AI what you don't want as well as what you do. "No pie charts" or "keep the color palette to two colors only" prevents the AI from defaulting to safe, generic choices.
🤝
You're the domain expert
The AI knows how to build things. You know what matters in your field. The best dashboards come from you bringing the "what matters" and the AI bringing the "how to show it."
🔍
Anomalies are features, not bugs
When your data has a weird spike or a suspicious gap, don't smooth it over — label it. The outlier is usually the most interesting thing in the dataset and the most memorable part of your presentation.
📝
The title is the insight
Change chart titles from descriptive ("Revenue by Quarter") to declarative ("Q3 revenue fell 18% — supply chain delays"). Your audience reads the title first. Make it do real work.

Are you ready to start? Checklist

You don't need all of these, but the more boxes you can tick, the smoother your first build will go.

✓
A clear question your dashboard will answer. If you can write it in one sentence, you're ready. If you need a paragraph, narrow it down first.
✓
Some data — even messy data. A CSV, a spreadsheet, a database export, even a table copy-pasted from a website. If you have nothing, ask AI to generate synthetic data for your domain.
✓
Access to an AI tool. Claude, ChatGPT, Gemini — any of the major models work. Claude tends to be strongest for long, structured code like dashboards.
✓
A browser to open the file. The output is a single HTML file. No server, no install, no deployment. Open it in Chrome or Firefox and it works.
✓
Patience for 2–3 iterations. The first version will have something wrong. That's normal. It's also fast to fix. Expect to go back and forth 3–5 times before it feels right.
→
Optional but helpful: a visual reference. Screenshot a dashboard you like — from Tableau Public, a SaaS product, anywhere. Share it with the AI and say "roughly this style, but for my data."
